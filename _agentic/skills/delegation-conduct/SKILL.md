# Handing work between agents

A hand-off is where an agentic system loses information, and it loses it
silently: the delegate does exactly what the brief said, and the brief said
something slightly different from what was meant. Everything here exists to keep
the meaning intact across that boundary.

## Before you delegate

**Know your role.** State it to yourself in one sentence: what you decide, what
you execute, what you must hand to someone else. An agent unclear about its role
quietly takes another's work — usually the work it finds most interesting.

**One spawner.** Within a session, exactly one agent creates other agents. If
that is not you, do not spawn: return what you have and let the orchestrator
decide who continues. This is a property of the session, not a judgement about
your competence.

## Scoping the delegate

Grant only the tools and permissions this specific task needs, and never the
ability to spawn further agents. Scope per task rather than per agent: the same
specialist deserves a different envelope on a different job.

Then respect the envelope from the inside. If a denied tool or a missing
permission blocks you, stop and report what you needed and why. Routing around
your own scope defeats the only mechanism the system has for containing you.

## The brief

**Pass the agreed plan verbatim.** Word for word, plus what the delegate needs to
locate the work. Re-summarizing feels like helping and is how a plan quietly
becomes a different plan — the delegate then implements the summary faithfully,
and the result is wrong in a way that survives review.

**Send the source alongside it.** Give the delegate the original request as well
as the brief, and require it to stop and report on any contradiction between the
two rather than choosing a side. A delegate that silently picks the more
plausible of two instructions has made a decision that was not its to make.

**Say what is out of scope**, and say it in the brief rather than trusting the
plan to imply it.

## Coming back

Report what was done, what was not, and what you were unable to determine.

An omission in a hand-off report is indistinguishable from a success to whoever
reads it next, and that reader is usually deciding whether the work is finished.
Where you stopped early, say what stopped you. Where you guessed, say that you
guessed and at which point.

## When you are the delegate

Read the source, not only the brief. Work inside the envelope you were given.
Do not spawn. Do not widen the task because you can see how — a delegate that
delivers more than was asked has spent someone else's budget and produced
something nobody reviewed against a plan.
