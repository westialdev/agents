# Oral communication

In a spoken assessment, communication is marked as a criterion of its own
(for example MYP Individuals and Societies criterion C: audience and
purpose, structure according to the task instructions, a reference list and
cited sources). A learner can know the content well and still score low
here. Knowing more does not fix it. Only practising the form does.

## The spoken answer contract

Agree this with the learner before the first practice answer, and hold
every answer to it, the same way `exam-conditions` holds a written answer
contract:

> signpost ("I'll talk about three things") → claim → evidence → **source
> named aloud** → link back to the question or key idea → one-sentence
> close

- **The opening presentation** follows the task instructions, part by
  part, in the order the task gives them. Write those parts out as a
  checklist from the official text, then mark each one present or missing.
- **Every piece of evidence is cited aloud, every time**: "In Book 1 of
  his *Geography*, Strabo says…", "according to the *Suda*, a tenth-century
  encyclopedia…". Saying "I read that…" or "it says online…" is an
  uncited claim. Count cited and uncited claims in each answer and report
  the count.
- **The reference list** is an object, not just a habit. If the task says
  or implies one (C(iii) "create a reference list"), the learner prepares
  it in the format the class uses, and brings it if the teacher allows.
  Ask in scope intake whether one must be handed in, shown or read out. If
  nobody knows, prepare it anyway. Check every entry: author, title, date
  and where it was found.
- **Audience and purpose**: formal register for a teacher, subject terms
  used correctly, no filler stacks ("like, basically, kind of"), answers
  that stop when they have answered. A list of facts with no point is
  weak structure, not strong content.
- **Length**: an answer of one sentence and an answer of three minutes both
  fail the audience. Time the opening against the real length when it is
  known.

## Marking

Mark the communication criterion on **every** spoken answer, not only on
the opening, with the evidence quoted: the signpost or its absence, the
cited and uncited count, and the close. A content-perfect answer with no
source named and no structure is not a high communication band. Say so
each time, and turn a repeated fault into a self-check question ("Who said
that, and where?").

## Know what you actually receive

Find out what the platform passes you before you judge delivery. Do not
assume either way. Some voice modes give the model the audio itself.
Others give it only a speech-recognition transcript, which drops pace,
volume, hesitation, and often the pauses and fillers. No voice mode shows
eye contact or posture. Judge only what reaches you, say in the report
which of these you received, and do not give a confident delivery band
from a transcript. Where you cannot observe delivery:

- ask the creator to listen to one full practice round, with this short
  checklist: opened with a plan? named a source for each claim? spoke to
  the listener rather than reciting? closed each answer? stayed within
  time?
- where the platform allows it, ask the learner to record one answer and
  play it back to themselves against the same checklist.

## Coaching

- The learner speaks every sentence. Model the contract on a different
  topic from theirs, never on their own material. A memorised script
  breaks at the first follow-up question.
- Drill the citation habit in short rounds: give a claim from their own
  research and ask them to say it again with its source. Repeat until it
  comes without prompting.
- Re-test structure with a fresh question under the contract, not by
  asking for the same answer again.
