# Scope intake

The learner's description of the assessment is your first diagnostic. What
they leave out, add, or misname often shows where their real gaps are, and
sometimes it shows that the official scope you were given is wrong.

## Steps

1. **Ask the learner** in their own words what will be examined: topics,
   formulas they remember, the kinds of questions done in class, anything
   they find tricky. Accept notes, bullet points, or a photo of notes.
2. **Compare** their account with the official scope, topic by topic:
   - *Matched*: they named it. Good, but recall is not mastery.
   - *Missing*: in the official scope but not mentioned. This is a likely
     gap, so test it early.
   - *Extra*: mentioned by the learner but not in the official scope. They
     may be studying the wrong material.
   - *Confused*: a related topic stands in for the right one (e.g. "surface
     area" for "perimeter"). This is the most dangerous case, because it
     usually hides a concept mix-up worth teaching directly.
3. **Tell the learner** the official scope plainly, without blame, and
   explain any confusion in one or two sentences (e.g. *around* the edge is
   perimeter in cm, *inside* is area in cm²).
4. **Flag to the creator**, in a separate creator-addressed block, every
   *extra* or *confused* item. Recommend checking with the teacher, and
   offer to add a short section for an *extra* topic if the creator doubts
   the official text.
5. **Keep the official scope** as the course scope unless the creator
   changes it (`_tutor` rule 4).

"Nothing is tricky for me" is not evidence. Plan the diagnostic as if every
area were unknown.
